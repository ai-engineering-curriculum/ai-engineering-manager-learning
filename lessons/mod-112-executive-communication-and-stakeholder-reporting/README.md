# mod-112-executive-communication-and-stakeholder-reporting: Executive Communication and Stakeholder Reporting

The AI engineering team does not ship to a leadership audience that reads eval reports. The compression step — from mod-104's eval registry, mod-107's postmortems, mod-110's budget artefacts, mod-111's partnership surface, into a weekly roll-up, a monthly deep-dive, and a quarterly exec-ready summary — is the AI Engineering Manager's own work. This module builds the discipline that makes the compression honest.

**Estimated effort:** 12 hours

## Learning objectives

- Produce weekly team roll-ups, monthly deep-dives, and quarterly exec-ready summaries that make model-uncertainty and release-gate reasoning legible to leadership that does not read eval reports directly.
- Defend AI investment asks — model-spend, labelling-data budget, headcount, tooling — with evidence that references empirical productivity work (Dell'Acqua et al. jagged frontier, Peng et al. copilot productivity) honestly, not as marketing.
- Narrate regressions honestly: when a release missed its eval delta, when an incident hit users, when a vendor upgrade regressed the product — publish the miss with the reason, not only the hits.
- Maintain demo discipline: the cherry-picked demo that pushes against the honest eval story is the single most common failure mode — this role's job is to prevent it in team artifacts, in exec packets, in external-facing communications.
- Position the role's next step on the ladder — upward toward director-of-AI-engineering / head-of-AI-engineering / chief-ai-officer (level 70), sideways toward ai-enablement-lead (level 45) or principal-ml-engineer / agentic-systems-architect (level 48) depending on growth preference — and the deliberate practice plan to get there.
