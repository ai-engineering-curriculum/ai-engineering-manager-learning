## Canonical executive-communication and leadership-writing reading (prerequisite)

The general executive-communication canon is `ai-augmented-team-manager` (level 40) territory and this module's prerequisite. The references below are the shape the AI engineering manager composes a specialisation on top of.

- Minto, Barbara — *The Pyramid Principle: Logic in Writing and Thinking*. Pearson, 3rd edition. [Pearson page](https://www.pearson.com/en-gb/subject-catalog/p/pyramid-principle-the-logic-in-writing-and-thinking/P200000005997) — the canonical reference for structured top-down executive writing; the SCQA (situation / complication / question / answer) frame underpins chapter 02's compression discipline.
- Berkun, Scott — *Making Things Happen: Mastering Project Management*. O'Reilly, 2008. [O'Reilly page](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/making-things-happen/9780596517717/) — specifically the chapters on communicating to executives and writing status reports with signal.
- Fournier, Camille — *The Manager's Path: A Guide for Tech Leaders Navigating Growth and Change*. O'Reilly, 2017. [O'Reilly page](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/the-managers-path/9781491973882/) — the "managing managers" and "senior leadership" chapters set the register the quarterly compresses to.
- Larson, Will — *An Elegant Puzzle: Systems of Engineering Management*. Stripe Press, 2019. [Stripe Press page](https://press.stripe.com/an-elegant-puzzle) — the "write a one-page strategy" and "executive communication" sections of the book.
- Hogan, Lara — *Resilient Management*. A Book Apart, 2019. [A Book Apart page](https://abookapart.com/products/resilient-management) — the chapter on communicating up with executives.
- Grove, Andrew S. — *High Output Management*. Vintage, 1995 (reissued 2015). [Vintage page](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/58715/high-output-management-by-andrew-s-grove/) — the operating-review and reporting-cadence substrate.

- Orosz, Gergely — *The Pragmatic Engineer* (Substack). [pragmaticengineer.com](https://www.pragmaticengineer.com/) — ongoing industry reading, specifically on engineering-leadership career arcs and the AI-engineering-manager role as it is emerging in large tech orgs.

## Empirical productivity research cited in chapter 03

The two anchor studies for the investment-ask citation discipline. Read in full before authoring an investment ask that cites them; the generalisability discussion in each paper is the specific content chapter 03's honest citation depends on.

- Dell'Acqua, Fabrizio; McFowland III, Edward; Mollick, Ethan; Lifshitz-Assaf, Hila; Kellogg, Katherine; Rajendran, Saran; Krayer, Lisa; Candelon, François; Lakhani, Karim R. — *Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier: Field Experimental Evidence of the Effects of AI on Knowledge Worker Productivity and Quality*. Harvard Business School Working Paper 24-013, September 2023. [HBS working paper page](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=64700) · [SSRN mirror](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4573321) — the "jagged frontier" study; specifically read the 19-percentage-point decline on outside-frontier tasks alongside the 40% quality gain on inside-frontier.
- Peng, Sida; Kalliamvakou, Eirini; Cihon, Peter; Demirer, Mert — *The Impact of AI on Developer Productivity: Evidence from GitHub Copilot*. arXiv:2302.06590, February 2023. [arXiv:2302.06590](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06590) — the Copilot RCT; specifically read the methods section to internalise the task scope (HTTP server implementation, bounded task, moderately experienced developers) before citing.

## Follow-on productivity research relevant to chapters 03 and 06

The two anchor studies are not the only measurements. The literature is evolving quickly; the honest investment ask reads breadth, not only the two most-cited papers.

- Microsoft Research — *The Effects of Generative AI on High Skilled Work: Evidence from Three Field Experiments with Software Developers*. 2024. [arXiv:2410.01331](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01331) — three RCTs across different populations; generally finds positive effects but with population-specific variation.
- METR — *Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity*. [metr.org blog post](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/) · [arXiv:2507.09089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089) — notable for finding experienced developers *felt* faster with AI but were measurably slower on specific complex-OSS work. Important counterweight when citing Peng et al. to avoid overclaiming.
- Mollick, Ethan — *Co-Intelligence: Living and Working with AI*. Portfolio, 2024. [Penguin Random House page](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/740739/co-intelligence-by-ethan-mollick/) — the practitioner-framing extension of Dell'Acqua et al.'s jagged-frontier reading. Cite as practitioner framing, not as empirical evidence.
- Mollick, Ethan — *One Useful Thing* (Substack). [oneusefulthing.org](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/) — the ongoing-reading surface for the jagged-frontier practice literature.
- GitHub — *Research: quantifying GitHub Copilot's impact on developer productivity and happiness*. 2022. [github.blog/2022-09-07](https://github.blog/2022-09-07-research-quantifying-github-copilots-impact-on-developer-productivity-and-happiness/) — vendor-authored, self-reported survey data. Useful for engagement; weaker as productivity evidence.
- McKinsey — *The Economic Potential of Generative AI*. 2023. [mckinsey.com](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/mckinsey-digital/our-insights/the-economic-potential-of-generative-ai-the-next-productivity-frontier) — macro-level estimates; useful for setting context, not for team-level calibration.
- Stack Overflow — *Annual Developer Survey*. [survey.stackoverflow.co](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/) — population-scale assistant-use and satisfaction data.
- Google DORA — *State of DevOps Report*. [dora.dev](https://dora.dev/) — the productivity-measurement discipline the AI-assisted-productivity question sits inside.
