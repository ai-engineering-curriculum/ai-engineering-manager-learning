# Positioning the Next Step on the Ladder

Every mod-108 growth plan this manager writes for an IC on their team has the same shape: current placement, trajectory target, dated commitments, anti-atrophy element, drift notes. The discipline is familiar; the manager has run it every cycle. The last chapter of this module applies the same discipline to the manager's own career surface.

The AI engineering manager at level 45 is at a branching point. Three next steps are visible on the broader ladder (mod-101 chapter 01 drew the neighbours):

- **Upward.** Director-of-AI-engineering, head-of-AI-engineering, chief AI officer at level 70, through the AI leadership arc.
- **Sideways toward enablement.** `ai-enablement-lead` at level 45 in the AI Workforce family — a lateral move into org-wide AI enablement rather than one engineering team.
- **Sideways toward staff-plus IC.** `principal-ml-engineer` or `agentic-systems-architect` at level 48 — a lateral return to the IC track after time in management, trading people leadership for architectural depth.

Each trajectory has a different artefact portfolio the manager needs to accumulate and a different deliberate-practice plan. This chapter builds each trajectory's plan the way mod-108 chapter 04 builds an IC's growth plan, and names the one plan the manager should write *now* even if the move is three years away.

## Motivation — three career-surface failures the written plan prevents

Three failure patterns that recur across managers who have been at level 45 for three or more years with no written plan.

- **The plateau that reads as "doing the job well."** The manager has been at L45 for three years. Every calibration cycle reads them as "performing at level," which is correct. The next step has never been explicitly named in a 1:1 with their own director; the artefact portfolio has not been accumulated with any specific next step in mind. The manager is overdue for a step up or a lateral but has no case to make, because the case was never deliberately constructed. The three years have been honest and productive; they have not been *trajectory-compounding*.
- **The sideways move that was the wrong sideways move.** The manager was considering the enablement-lead role but never explicitly walked through what the role entails against what they currently love about their work. A reorganisation presented the lateral opportunity; they took it; six months in they discovered the parts of their current role they most valued (the eval-gate chair, the release-triad sign-off, the daily reading of the trace corpus) are absent from the new role. Mod-108 chapter 05 of *The Manager's Path* names this pattern as the IC-to-manager trap; the same shape exists at L45 → sideways. The written plan is the antidote.
- **The directorship absorbed by someone who was more externally visible but less internally substantive.** A peer at a sibling team was more active in externally-facing forums — conference talks, podcast appearances, the industry Slack community. The directorship was awarded to them; the manager who had done more substantive work on their team's engineering surface lost the comparison because the external visibility was what the directorship selection read on. The artefact portfolio for upward trajectory is specifically about *visibility at the right altitude*, not only internal substance. Chapter 05 of this module teaches the external-artefact discipline honestly; this chapter composes it into the career trajectory.

Each failure is a specific absence of plan. The manager can — and should — write the plan that prevents each.

## Core concept 1 — the manager's own growth-plan template

The template below mirrors mod-108 chapter 04's six-section IC growth-plan template, re-targeted to the manager's own career surface.

```
Role: ai-engineering-manager         Level: L45
Team: [current team context]         Years at L45: [number]
Date: [today]                        Next review: [date]

1. Current placement.
   Shape (upward / sideways-enablement / sideways-staff-IC — pick one as the primary 
   target, name the fallback as secondary). Last calibration grade. The three or four 
   artefacts that most define your current contribution. The one conversation with 
   your own manager that is honest about the trajectory target.

2. Trajectory target for the next 18 months.
   The specific next role and family. The named organisation (current employer or 
   external). The level target. The two or three specific capabilities you need to 
   demonstrate for that role that you have not yet demonstrated at this level.

3. Specific commitments, with sequencing and timebox.
   Three to five dated commitments in the next cycle that build evidence toward the 
   trajectory. Each has a named sponsor (your own manager, a peer at the target 
   level, a staff-plus IC, a mentor, an external coach). Each has a check-in date.

4. Anti-atrophy element.
   The specific practice that keeps the engineering substance of the role sharp 
   while the management substance expands. For an L45 moving upward, this is the 
   deliberate loop against mod-108 chapter 05's three-loop framework (deliberate 
   practice, reading, rotation), specialised to the manager's own craft.

5. Credential-honesty element.
   Any externally-facing artefact plans — a conference talk, a blog post, an 
   industry publication, a board advisorship — with the inside read-out named. 
   External activity without an inside read-out is theatre; this is chapter 05 
   of this module, applied to the manager's own career.

6. Drift notes.
   The frontier-model shift that may re-shape the role surface over the plan window. 
   The organisational shift that may close a particular next-step path. The honest 
   assessment of which part of the plan is contingent on an external condition.
```

Six sections, one to two pages, co-authored with the manager's own director, updated at review cycles. The same discipline applied to the IC's plan applies here; the application is the chapter.

## Core concept 2 — trajectory 1, upward toward director / head / CAIO

The most common upward arc. The level-70 CAIO (mod-101 chapter 01) is the executive-altitude destination; director-of-AI-engineering (L55 or L60 depending on the org's ladder) and head-of-AI-engineering (L60 or L65) are the intermediate stops. Not every organisation has all three rungs; the plan reads against the specific organisation's ladder.

### The capability shift

Four capabilities the upward trajectory requires that L45 does not demand at the same altitude.

- **Multi-team reading surface.** A director reads on three to seven teams, not one. The artefact surface is the quarterly across the portfolio, not the quarterly for one team. The calibration discipline runs across teams; the cross-team roadmap integration becomes the planning surface.
- **External-narrative authorship.** A director or head is a face of the function externally. The analyst briefings, the industry podcast appearances, the hiring-market positioning, the regulator-facing representation — the director authors these at a different altitude than the manager. Chapter 05 of this module's demo-discipline practice composes directly into this capability.
- **Capital-allocation authority at a higher ceiling.** The director approves asks the manager assembles; the head sets the envelope the director allocates within; the CAIO defends the envelope to the board. The investment-ask fluency chapter 03 teaches is the foundation; the authority surface grows one or two orders of magnitude.
- **Executive-peer fluency.** The director is in rooms with the CFO, the CTO, the COO, legal counsel, the head of product, and the head of AI governance as peers, not as customers. The register, the asks, the trade-offs are different. Chapter 07 of mod-111 (the four deferrals) is a necessary read for this altitude.

### The artefact portfolio

Four artefacts the upward trajectory accumulates. Each has a dated target in the plan.

1. **A full fiscal year of honest quarterly exec-ready summaries.** The chapter 02 quarterly discipline run over four consecutive quarters, with the honest miss ledger intact. A director-hiring loop reads the fourth quarter's quarterly as evidence of the manager's reporting substance. One quarter of good quarterlies is not evidence; four is.
2. **Two cross-team artefacts at the function level.** An eval-registry standard adopted across sibling engineering teams, a shared release-gate policy across the AI-engineering function, a jointly-authored postmortem narrative with a peer manager's team — any artefact that evidences influence beyond the current team. The staff-plus IC partner (agentic-systems-architect or principal-ml-engineer) is often the co-author; chapter 04 of mod-108 teaches the mechanics.
3. **One externally-facing credential piece with an inside read-out.** A conference talk, a blog series, a podcast appearance, an invited industry whitepaper. The *specific* read-out — what came back to the team's handbook, what a peer team learned, what a candidate was recruited from it — is the credential-honesty move from chapter 05. External activity without this read-out is theatre.
4. **A deputy-development arc on the team.** At least one IC on the team that the manager has grown to L40 or beyond, with a documented trajectory; at least one senior IC the manager has sponsored for staff-plus consideration. A director-hiring loop reads this as evidence of leverage. A manager who has produced no promotions in three years is a manager whose upward case has no leverage story.

### The deliberate-practice loops

Three loops run across the plan window.

- **The reading loop.** One hour per week on industry research — the empirical productivity literature (chapter 03's references and their follow-ups), the AI governance regulatory surface (EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, the emerging regulatory regimes),<!-- needs-research: verify current scope of ISO/IEC 42001 adoption and any superseding standards --> the operating-cadence writing (mod-109's references plus the engineering-leadership canon), and the specific director-at-scale writing (Will Larson's *Staff Engineer* and *An Elegant Puzzle*, Camille Fournier's *The Manager's Path* chapter on directors, Gergely Orosz's *The Software Engineer's Guidebook* chapter on EM-to-director transitions).<!-- needs-research: verify current editions and any superseding publications -->
- **The shadowing loop.** A standing monthly 1:1 or 2:1 with the manager's own director, scoped explicitly to the director-level work; a quarterly observation shadow in a cross-function forum the director attends (steering committee, senior leadership offsite). Shadowing is not status-seeking; it is practice against the altitude's register.
- **The external-forum loop.** One externally-facing engagement per quarter — a podcast appearance, a Slack-community AMA, a meetup talk, a published blog post. The credential-honesty element (chapter 05) is enforced; theatre is caught.

### The common failure for the upward trajectory

The upward trajectory most commonly fails for one of two reasons. First: the manager has accumulated internal substance but no external credential — the hiring loop reads them as "strong manager but hard to pick out of the crowd." Second: the manager has accumulated external credentials without the internal substance — the hiring loop reads them as "visible but hollow when you ask them how the eval surface actually works."

Both failures are easy to notice in advance. A manager with four quarters of honest quarterlies, two cross-team artefacts, a deputy-development arc, *and* one honest externally-facing credential piece is in neither failure mode. A manager missing any one of the four is in one of them.

## Core concept 3 — trajectory 2, sideways toward enablement-lead (L45)

The lateral move into AI Workforce — the `ai-enablement-lead` role at the same level, running org-wide AI enablement rather than one engineering team. A real lateral, not a step down; the authority profile is different, the customer set is different, the artefact set is different.

### The capability shift

- **The customer expands to every function.** The enablement-lead's customer is not one engineering team; it is every team in the organisation — product, operations, customer success, marketing, finance, legal. The register expands accordingly. The partnership surface is horizontally wider and shallower rather than vertically deeper.
- **The authority surface is softer.** The enablement-lead does not sign off on releases; they do not chair the eval gate; they do not run the on-call rotation. The authority is persuasive — a programme, a rollout, a training arc, a usage-policy co-authoring with legal and HR. A manager who draws most of their satisfaction from the sign-off authority of the engineering role should test whether the softer authority still provides it.
- **The engineering substance recedes.** The daily reading of the trace corpus is gone. The release-gate chair is gone. The model-watch meeting is gone. The role moves from building AI features to enabling AI use across functions. If the engineering substance was the manager's primary draw, the lateral is the wrong one and the plan should name this honestly.

### The artefact portfolio

Three artefacts worth accumulating during the current L45 role window if the lateral is seriously being considered.

1. **A cross-functional partnership artefact.** Co-authoring an organisation-wide AI usage policy with legal and HR, chairing an AI steering committee's engineering-side contribution, co-leading an all-hands AI readiness session. Mod-111 chapter 06 teaches the sibling-partnership mechanics with the enablement-lead; the manager who has done this once as a partner is a manager the hiring loop can read as capable of being in that role.
2. **A training-and-enablement artefact.** Running a training arc for ICs on the team (mod-108 chapter 05's anti-atrophy loop is the base), extending it to engineers on a sibling team, producing the materials in a shareable form. The artefact evidences the enablement muscle.
3. **An AI literacy artefact for non-engineers.** Partnering with the current enablement-lead (or whoever runs the function) on a non-engineering audience — a session for the product org, a brown-bag for the customer-success team, an exec brief on AI capability shifts. The non-engineering audience is where the enablement-lead role lives; a manager who has only ever communicated AI to engineers is a manager the lateral hiring loop will read as untested.

### The deliberate-practice loops

- **The non-engineering-audience reading loop.** Reading in the AI governance and workforce-strategy literature — the NIST AI RMF, the AI Workforce Alliance research, the Deloitte / Gartner / McKinsey AI-adoption surveys<!-- needs-research: cite the most-current industry surveys on AI adoption outside engineering functions --> — alongside the engineering-leadership canon. The register of the destination role is the register of the reading.
- **The cross-functional 1:1 loop.** Standing 1:1s with partners outside engineering — the head of product, the head of customer success, the head of legal — scoped explicitly to shared AI-adoption surfaces, not to specific engineering asks. Practice against the destination role's register.
- **The "which parts of my current work would I miss" loop.** A written exercise on a quarterly cadence, following mod-108 chapter 05's IC-to-manager pivot discipline but inverted — which parts of the manager's engineering role would they lose, and does losing them read as a loss or as a release. The written loop is honest in a way the conversation with the director is not; it surfaces whether the lateral is the right one before the lateral is taken.

## Core concept 4 — trajectory 3, sideways toward principal-ml-engineer / agentic-systems-architect (L48)

The return-to-IC trajectory. The level-48 staff-plus IC roles (`principal-ml-engineer` or `agentic-systems-architect` from mod-101 chapter 01) are where a manager with a technical substrate goes when they want depth over breadth. The level is higher than the current L45 (the staff-plus IC track runs higher than the first-line-manager track in most ladders); the authority is deeper on architecture and methodology and narrower on people.

### The capability shift

- **People-management recedes.** The staff-plus IC does not have direct reports. The 1:1 cadence, the calibration discipline, the hiring loop — all of them mod-108 territory — stop being the manager's first-order work. A manager who draws significant satisfaction from the people-growth arc (and most do) should test whether they will sustain in a role where it is secondary.
- **Technical depth expands.** The staff-plus IC leads architectural decisions, methodology memos, cross-team technical standards. The manager who has been at L45 for three years may have maintained the trace-reading and model-watch muscle; most have not. The eighteen-month plan window is partly a return-to-depth window.
- **The influence is horizontal.** The staff-plus IC influences other teams' engineers through artefacts (ADRs, methodology handbooks, design docs, tool-contract specs) rather than through managerial authority. The *architect's path* is specifically about this shift.

### The artefact portfolio

Three artefacts to accumulate before the lateral.

1. **Depth-of-craft evidence.** A substantial architectural artefact — an ADR adopted across the function, a methodology memo on an AI-engineering-specific craft shift (e.g., structured outputs discipline, the eval-registry convention), a jointly-authored design doc with the current staff-plus IC partner. One artefact that reads as staff-plus-caliber IC work, dated.
2. **An externally-cited technical artefact.** A conference talk at the staff-plus altitude, a blog post the wider engineering community has cited, an open-source contribution the community has read. The staff-plus hiring loop is specifically about external technical visibility; the manager's external surface pivots from managerial to technical.
3. **The deputy-handoff plan.** A written plan for who takes the team if the lateral is taken, with the development arc for that deputy already in flight. A manager who cannot name a credible deputy is a manager whose lateral is also a team disruption; the plan reads honestly about this.

### The deliberate-practice loops

- **The trace-reading loop.** A standing practice slot (an hour or two per week) actually reading traces, writing eval cases, debugging prompts, and reviewing code at the IC craft depth. Mod-108 chapter 05's anti-atrophy framing applied to the manager's own return to IC.
- **The methodology-authorship loop.** A monthly writing commitment — a methodology memo, an ADR, a technical blog post — on a specific AI-engineering craft topic. The writing rebuilds the muscle that managerial writing displaces.
- **The IC-partnership loop.** Standing 1:1s or 2:1s with the current staff-plus IC (agentic-systems-architect or principal-ml-engineer) scoped to shared technical work; pair-writing on a methodology memo; co-authoring an ADR. Practice against the destination role with the destination role's incumbent.

## Core concept 5 — the one plan to write now

Of the three trajectories, which one to plan against? The manager writes *the primary trajectory* in section 2 of the growth-plan template, names *the secondary* in the same section, and runs the deliberate-practice loops *most relevant to the primary*. The plan is dated and revisited every six months.

The honest selection move: four questions, written answers, fifteen minutes.

1. **What part of your current work do you most look forward to?** The eval-gate chair? The investment-ask quarterly? The non-engineer brown-bag? The deep trace read? The answer biases toward one trajectory and away from another.
2. **What part do you dread?** The calibration meeting? The DPIA sign-off chase? The engineering postmortem? The externally-facing podcast? The dread signal is as informative as the look-forward signal.
3. **What have you accumulated artefacts for *without planning to*?** Patterns in the artefact set over the last two years hint at where your work has pulled you. If your Google Drive has three draft industry blog posts and no architectural memos, the pull is probably external-facing. If it has two methodology memos and no blog posts, the pull is technical-IC.
4. **Who do you most want to spend the next eighteen months beside?** The staff-plus IC partner? The enablement-lead sibling? The director you report to? The honest answer biases.

The four answers do not decide the trajectory; they reveal which trajectory the manager is already partway toward. The plan then makes the partial trajectory deliberate.

A worked-example snippet of the plan's section 2:

```
Trajectory target for the next 18 months:
  Primary — upward to director-of-AI-engineering at current employer. Level target 
  L55 by Q2 2028. Specific capabilities to demonstrate: multi-team reading (currently 
  one team; director reads three to seven); external-narrative authorship (currently 
  no credential piece; target one talk and one blog series in the plan window); 
  deputy development (currently zero senior ICs grown to L40; target one by Q4 2027).
  Secondary — sideways to agentic-systems-architect at L48 if the director window 
  closes at current employer. Would re-open the lateral conversation Q1 2028 if the 
  upward path is blocked.

  Named sponsor — my own director (standing monthly 1:1 scoped to this plan); 
  secondary sponsor — the staff-plus agentic-systems-architect on the current team 
  (pair-reviewing the two cross-team artefacts below).
```

Specific. Dated. Named. Secondary is explicit. The plan is not a wish; it is a schedule.

## What good looks like

- The manager has a written growth plan, updated at the same cadence as the IC growth plans they author for the team.
- The plan names a primary trajectory and a secondary trajectory specifically; neither is "whatever opens up."
- Dated commitments with named sponsors are current; the next one has a check-in date.
- The deliberate-practice loops (reading, shadowing, external-forum, trace-reading, cross-functional, methodology-authorship — the relevant subset for the primary trajectory) are on the manager's calendar.
- An honest annual review against the plan occurs; the plan is updated or the trajectory is revised.

## What bad looks like

- No written plan. The next-step conversation surfaces as a reorganisation surprise or as a passed-over promotion.
- The plan names three trajectories as equally likely with no selection discipline; the manager ends up accumulating no artefact portfolio consistently.
- The deliberate-practice loops are aspirational, not calendared; six months pass without a reading hour or a shadowing session.
- The artefact portfolio accumulates by default — whichever artefact the current team's work most pulled for — rather than by deliberate planning against the trajectory.

## Summary

- The AI engineering manager at L45 is at a branching point: upward toward director / head / CAIO, sideways toward enablement-lead at L45, sideways toward principal-ml-engineer or agentic-systems-architect at L48.
- Each trajectory has a specific capability shift, artefact portfolio, and deliberate-practice loop set. The manager's own growth plan mirrors the IC growth plan from mod-108 chapter 04; the same discipline, applied to self.
- The one plan to write now is the primary trajectory with a secondary named; the four-question honest-selection exercise reveals which trajectory the manager is already partway toward.
- The chapter closes the module — and the track. The reporting discipline the module teaches (three cadences, honest regression narration, demo discipline, cited evidence) composes into the credibility substrate on which each trajectory's next step is read.

## End of module

The module is complete. The team's reporting surface has three cadences, four honesty moves, and a demo discipline. The manager's own trajectory has a written plan. The compression that is this role's work — the engineering surface into leadership-legible artefacts, the ICs' craft into a function-level signal, the current L45 into a next step — is explicit, dated, and reviewable.
