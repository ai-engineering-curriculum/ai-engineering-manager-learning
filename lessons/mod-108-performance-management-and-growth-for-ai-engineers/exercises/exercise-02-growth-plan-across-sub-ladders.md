# Exercise 02: Growth Plan Across Sub-Ladders

**Estimated effort:** 2 hours · **Output:** four completed growth plans, one for each of the four named trajectories in chapter 04, co-authored (or as-if-co-authored) with an IC on your team (or realistic composites).

## Objective

Author four full growth plans that demonstrate the manager-owned artefact from chapter 04 at depth. Each plan covers a different trajectory: level-up within shape, cross-shape rotation, tech-lead stretch, IC ↔ manager pivot. Each plan is complete enough that the next cycle's review packet (chapter 03) can read directly off it.

A peer AI Engineering Manager, reading any one of your four plans, should be able to say what the IC is working toward, who is sponsoring it, what specific commitments move it forward in the next cycle, and what the anti-atrophy and credential-honesty elements are.

## Prerequisites

- Chapter 04 — the growth-plan template; four named trajectories; cross-shape rotation mechanics; the IC ↔ manager pivot with Phase 0 / 1 / 2.
- Chapter 05 — the three loops (deliberate practice, reading, rotation) each plan's anti-atrophy element draws from.
- Chapter 07 — the credential-honesty element each plan may or may not carry.
- The level rubric from exercise 01, or an equivalent rubric already published on your team.
- Four real ICs (or realistic composites) at the right levels and shapes for the four trajectories.

## Problem statement

Four growth plans, each roughly one page (200–400 words), in your team's growth-plan format (markdown doc per IC, or whatever substrate your 1:1 shared docs use). Each plan has the six sections from chapter 04 core concept 2:

1. Current placement (shape, level, last-review artefact-class grid).
2. Trajectory target for the next 12 months (named: Trajectory 1, 2, 3, or 4; one of the four).
3. Specific commitments, with sequencing and timebox (3–5 commitments, dated, sponsored).
4. Anti-atrophy element (the three loops, right-sized to level; chapter 05).
5. Credential-honesty element (if the plan includes external artefacts; chapter 07).
6. Drift notes (any technology-drift considerations; chapter 06).

### Plan 1 — Trajectory 1: level-up within shape (25 minutes)

An IC growing toward the next level anchor within their current shape. Pick an IC and a specific trajectory (L30 → L40 is the most common; L20 → L30 or L40 → L48 are also valid). The plan should name specific artefact-class gaps from the rubric and the specific commitments that close them. The staff-plus IC sponsor (for L30 → L40 or L40 → L48) must be named.

### Plan 2 — Trajectory 2: cross-shape rotation (25 minutes)

An IC rotating from one shape to another for a cycle or two. Pick one of the common patterns from chapter 04 (applied → eval, applied → agentic, RAG → applied, agentic → senior agentic, fine-tune → applied, infra → applied/agentic). The plan should name the three phases (pairing, scoped ownership, placement decision), the two sponsors (sending manager + receiving specialist), and the specific scope the IC will own in each phase.

### Plan 3 — Trajectory 3: tech-lead stretch (25 minutes)

An IC taking a tech-lead role on a specific sub-system without (yet) a formal level change. Pick a sub-system on your team — the agent's planning loop, the eval registry's convention layer, the retrieval substrate's corpus contract, etc. The plan should name the design-document commitment, the architecture-review chairing commitment, the cross-team artefact commitment (ADR, tool-contract spec, methodology memo), the staff-plus IC partner, and the incident-command rotation.

### Plan 4 — Trajectory 4: IC ↔ manager pivot — Phase 0 (25 minutes)

An L40 IC considering the management track. The plan is deliberately slow here — Phase 0 is the honest trajectory walk-through, before any commitment. The plan should name:

- The specific "which parts of your current work would you miss?" questions to walk through.
- The co-sponsor from `ai-augmented-team-manager` (level 40) for the management-side content.
- The two or three proto-management experiences that would form Phase 1 if Phase 0 lands "yes" (stretch assignment: lead a two-person pod, run two L20s' 1:1s under observation, co-chair the hiring loop, co-run the retrospective).
- The explicit **return clause** — the IC's path back to IC if Phase 1 lands "I don't want this."

If no L40 on your team is currently considering the pivot, write the plan as a composite — a plausible IC and a plausible pivot scenario.

## Requirements

- Four growth plans, each a complete markdown document with the six sections from chapter 04.
- Each plan names a specific IC (or realistic composite) at a specific shape and level from exercise 01's rubric.
- Each plan has 3–5 commitments, each dated with a timebox and a named sponsor if someone beyond the direct manager is involved.
- Each plan has an anti-atrophy element right-sized to the IC's level (chapter 05 table).
- Each plan that includes external-artefact commitments has a credential-honesty element with the specific inside read-out.
- Each plan has drift notes (one or two lines minimum, even if "no major drift expected this cycle").
- Plan 2 (cross-shape rotation) names the three phases, two sponsors, and a specific scope per phase.
- Plan 3 (tech-lead stretch) names the sub-system, the design-document commitment, the cross-team artefact, the staff-plus partner.
- Plan 4 (IC ↔ manager pivot) names the Phase 0 walk-through, the co-sponsor, the Phase 1 stretch options, and the return clause.
- Each plan is between 200 and 400 words. Shorter plans will be thin on commitments; longer plans drift toward essay.

## Starter guidance

- Write the Current Placement section first on each plan. The artefact-class grid from exercise 01 is the base; everything downstream reads against it.
- For Plan 1, pick an IC whose grid has one or two specific gap rows at the next level. Those rows are the plan's commitments. If the IC is "at" on every row at the next level, they are not Trajectory 1 — they are overdue for promotion and the growth plan is a Trajectory 3 or 4 instead.
- For Plan 2, spend time on who the receiving specialist is and what the rotation contract between you and their manager will look like. Rotations fail because the two-manager contract never got written; the exercise is to write it.
- For Plan 3, the tech-lead stretch, test your commitment set against the "senior-IC trap" from chapter 04. If the commitments do not include cross-team scope, the stretch collapses into "senior IC doing their own work well," which is Trajectory 1, not Trajectory 3. Rewrite.
- For Plan 4, resist the temptation to pre-sell the pivot. Phase 0's job is to let the IC decide; a plan that reads as a pitch for management is a plan that will produce a quarter of productivity loss on the team and a dented engineer six months later. The walk-through questions should be hard; the return clause should be specific.
- For every plan: the anti-atrophy element is mandatory, even when the plan is otherwise light. Chapter 05 is specific about why. If you catch yourself cutting the anti-atrophy element for space, something else on the plan is over-scoped.
- For every plan: the credential-honesty element is optional but, if present, must include the inside read-out. "Give a conference talk" without an inside read-out is theatre. "Give a conference talk on the HITL decision memo; the talk pulls the memo into publishable shape and we add it to the team handbook" is useful growth.
- For every plan: the drift notes section is the entry to chapter 06's calibration. A plan without drift notes is a plan the drift-fair review cannot read against; chapter 06 is specific.
- Share the plans (or composite equivalents) with the IC's prospective sponsor before finalising. A plan the staff-plus partner has read is a plan the staff-plus partner will stand behind when calibration asks.

## Acceptance criteria

- Four plans produced, each a complete markdown document in your team's growth-plan format.
- Each plan covers exactly one of the four named trajectories; the four plans cover all four.
- Each plan has all six sections from chapter 04 core concept 2.
- Each plan carries 3–5 dated commitments with named sponsors where applicable.
- Each plan has an anti-atrophy element right-sized to level per the chapter-05 table.
- Each plan with external-artefact commitments has a credential-honesty element with inside read-outs specified.
- Plan 2 has a written cross-team rotation contract (three paragraphs minimum) linking in the sending and receiving managers.
- Plan 3 names cross-team scope that is more than "doing the work well."
- Plan 4 explicitly names the return clause.
- Each plan is 200–400 words.
- A peer manager can read any plan and answer: who is the IC, what is the trajectory, what moves next cycle, who is the sponsor, how will drift-fair review grade them.

## Stretch goals

- **Full team coverage.** Produce the growth plan for every IC on your team, not only the four trajectory exemplars. Most ICs will be Trajectory 1 or 3; one or two will be 2 or 4. The full set is the input to the next calibration meeting (chapter 06).
- **12-month arc.** For Plan 1 (level-up within shape), extend to a 12-month view: what are the three cycle-by-cycle commitments, what is the planned promotion window, what are the artefact-class targets at each cycle end. The 12-month arc is the manager's forecasting artefact.
- **Reverse-pivot plan.** Produce a fifth plan for a manager-to-IC return (chapter 04's manager → IC direction). This trajectory is under-named and worth having on file for the manager who tests the role and prefers IC.
- **Cross-team rotation contract library.** If Plan 2's rotation is realistic, produce the full three-paragraph contract and the monthly joint-1:1 cadence. Pair it with the two-manager read-out schedule. The library is reusable across future rotations.
- **IC-facing version.** Write the IC-facing version of one plan (the words you would share with the IC for their own review). The IC version omits anything in the manager-facing version that the IC should not see (e.g., manager's private concern about halo risk the 1:1 diagnostic has not yet confirmed) and reframes any manager-side language ("we are watching for the frontier-assistant halo risk") into IC-side language ("we are investing in the owned-judgment-under-throughput practice loop").
- **Sponsorship-map read.** Across the four plans, who are the sponsors? Is the staff-plus IC partner being asked to sponsor three of four plans? If yes, the plans are over-depending on one person and a conversation with that person about load is due; chapter 09 (team shape and operating cadence) is the broader frame.
