# Exercise 05: Data Flywheel Plan and Budget

**Estimated effort:** 4 hours · **Output:** one data-flywheel plan (2–3 pages) + a rater-calibration standard + a one-page labelling budget suitable for a finance review + a quarterly review agenda

## Objective

Produce the four artefacts the manager-owned data flywheel (R13) depends on: a written **flywheel plan** for one feature covering all four stations (Capture → Label → Version → Consume), a **rater-calibration standard** that governs who may label and how inter-rater reliability is measured, a defensible **labelling budget** that can be read by finance / leadership in one page, and the **quarterly review agenda** that runs the flywheel memo chapter 07 prescribes.

## Prerequisites

- Chapter 07 (`Owning the Data Flywheel (R13)`) as the main source.
- Chapter 04 (`The Three Registries`) for the versioning station.
- Exercise 01 for the feature's commissioning brief (same feature preferred, so the exercises compose).
- A rough handle on your organisation's budgeting mechanism — product vs. platform vs. compliance funding patterns.

## Problem statement

Produce four artefacts for one specific feature.

### Artefact 1 — The data-flywheel plan (90 minutes)

A 2–3 page markdown document. One section per station (Capture / Label / Version / Consume) plus an opening summary and a closing "how the loop closes" paragraph.

#### Section 0 — Summary (10 minutes)

One paragraph. What this flywheel feeds (evals, judge calibration, fine-tune if applicable), its intended throughput (cases labelled per quarter), and the single sentence that defines success ("the flywheel is working if quarterly suite growth matches the quarterly risk-surface change").

#### Section 1 — Capture (20 minutes)

For each source, name what you capture, from where, at what sampling policy, with what PII posture:

- **Production traces** — stratification (audience, surface, tool-use vs. no-tool, length bucket); sampling percentage; flag-triggered priority queue (safety-rule hits, user downvotes, support escalations).
- **User feedback** — thumbs, comments, ratings; how they attach to traces; retention.
- **Support follow-ups** — the CS-to-eval handoff; who routes a ticket to the labelling queue; cadence.
- **Red-team exercises** — the standing cadence (per `mod-106`); the obligation that every finding becomes a versioned case within N days.
- **Synthetic generation** — if you use it, which cases, generated by what; the human-review gate; the diversity-collapse risk management.

Name a **capture owner** (one person — usually the applied engineer plus the eval engineer).

#### Section 2 — Label (25 minutes)

- **Streams** — the mix of in-house engineer / in-house domain expert / vendor / crowd. For each, approximate throughput and cost shape.
- **Tooling** — the labelling platform (e.g., `Argilla`, `Label Studio`, `Prodigy`, observability-platform-native). One platform; adopt-and-defend.
- **PII and security posture** — what data a vendor sees vs. what stays in-house; DPA requirements; redaction pipeline if needed.
- **Review overhead** — the quality-control auditor who confirms a sample of labels. Budget 10–20% of labelling capacity.
- **Named owner** — the `ai-eval-engineer` (usually) or dedicated data-quality owner.

#### Section 3 — Version (15 minutes)

Reference the dataset-registry standard from exercise 03. Name the bump cadence (weekly during active growth, quarterly at steady state), the changelog discipline, the access controls (holdout, PII), and the retention policy (aligned with upstream trace retention).

#### Section 4 — Consume (15 minutes)

For each consumer, name the cadence:

- **Eval suite** — which new cases went where, which regressions last quarter were caught by newly-added cases.
- **Judge calibration set** — the refresh cadence (quarterly or on judge-model upgrade), the owner.
- **Fine-tune pipeline** — if applicable; the provenance-and-licensing gate before data enters training.

#### Section 5 — How the loop closes (5 minutes)

One paragraph: what labelled data this quarter changes about what you capture next quarter. A flywheel without this closing is four independent pipelines.

### Artefact 2 — The rater-calibration standard (45 minutes)

A 1–2 page markdown document. Sections:

- **The guideline reference** — a one-paragraph description of the rubric guidelines (link to them); when they were last reviewed; who owns edits.
- **Onboarding calibration test** — size, composition, pass threshold (Cohen's κ against gold-standard labels), what happens when a rater fails (re-training; cannot label production data until re-tested).
- **Ongoing inter-rater reliability** — the double-labelling percentage (5–10% typical), the aggregate metric reported (κ for pairs, Fleiss' κ or Krippendorff's α for multi-rater), the quarterly reporting cadence.
- **The calibration-owner role** — named human. Not "the team." Responsibilities: guideline edits, onboarding tests, quarterly κ reporting, rater re-certification.
- **Action triggers** — what happens when reliability falls below a threshold: guideline tightening, rater re-training, task redesign.

### Artefact 3 — The one-page labelling budget (45 minutes)

A one-page document pitched at finance / leadership. Sections:

- **Capacity** — hours per quarter the team (and vendors) can dedicate, broken down by stream (engineer / domain-expert / vendor). State the expected labels-per-hour by stream.
- **Dollars** — vendor spend (per-label cost ranges × planned volume), platform licensing, labelling-tool infrastructure. Total quarterly dollar figure with a confidence range.
- **Review overhead** — 10–20% of labelling capacity for QC audit, accounted separately.
- **Budget source** — product budget / AI-platform budget / compliance budget / blend. State the chosen source and the rationale (chapter 07 covers the trade-offs).
- **Return on labelling** — what this quarter's labelling spend is expected to produce (eval cases added, judge recalibration cycles, fine-tune data readiness). Quantify where possible.
- **Trade-off ladder** — one paragraph describing the "if the budget is cut by 20%, we would cut X" ladder. The piece finance most wants.

Expect this artefact to be read in a quarterly finance review. Keep it executive-grade: tight, defensible, with trade-offs explicit.

### Artefact 4 — The quarterly review agenda (30 minutes)

A one-page agenda for the flywheel's quarterly review (chapter 07). 60–90 minutes. Sections, in order, each with time-box:

1. **Throughput** — cases captured / labelled / landed, by source.
2. **Quality** — inter-rater reliability trend, judge–human agreement trend, raters re-certified this quarter.
3. **Consumption** — new eval cases that caught regressions; judge calibration refreshes; fine-tune data releases.
4. **Budget** — spend vs. plan; overrun or underspend explained.
5. **Red-team obligation audit** — every red-team finding this quarter has a versioned corpus entry. Open items named.
6. **Changes** — guideline edits, new rubrics, retention / PII posture changes.
7. **Next quarter targets** — case counts, new rubric areas, re-calibration schedule.

Name the chair (you), the participants (eval engineer, data-quality owner, applied engineer, safety peer if red-team is on the agenda), and the memo format (one-page summary attached to the quarterly engineering review per `mod-109`).

## Requirements

- The flywheel plan is 2–3 pages, has all four stations plus the loop-closure paragraph. A station without content fails the exercise; mark "N/A because …" if genuinely out of scope.
- Every station names a human owner by name (or `[placeholder: named at team formation]` plus a date).
- The rater-calibration standard names: a guideline owner, an onboarding pass threshold with a specific metric, an ongoing double-labelling percentage, and a named calibration owner.
- The labelling budget is one page, pitched at finance / leadership, with a trade-off ladder.
- The quarterly review agenda is one page, has at least seven agenda items including the red-team obligation audit, and names a chair + participants + memo output.
- The red-team obligation is enforceable: a specific commitment that every red-team finding becomes a versioned corpus entry within a specific number of days.

## Starter guidance

- Start with Artefact 3 (the budget). It is the hardest artefact to produce because it forces concrete numbers. Once you have the budget, the flywheel-plan throughput figures (Artefact 1 Section 0) and the rater-calibration throughput (Artefact 2) are consistent with it.
- For the rater-calibration standard: if your team has never measured inter-rater reliability before, pilot it on a small sample before writing the production standard. A standard that demands κ ≥ 0.75 is useful only if the team's current κ is near that number; if it is 0.5, aim for κ ≥ 0.6 with a plan to tighten.
- The red-team obligation is the single most-skipped line in the whole module. Write it as a hard commitment: every finding becomes a versioned corpus entry within N days of the exercise. Pair the obligation with a tracked ticket per finding. Chapter 07 goes deeper.
- If your organisation's budget pattern is immature (no labelling line yet), this exercise is often the first time anybody has quantified the real cost. Leadership frequently underestimates the cost until it is in front of them in this form. The hard work is converting the written plan into the budget conversation that creates the line.
- The quarterly review is the beat that keeps the flywheel honest. Pick a date in the first 10 days of the quarter for last-quarter's review; pin it on the calendar now.

## Acceptance criteria

- Flywheel plan covers Capture / Label / Version / Consume + loop-closure; owners named; red-team obligation enforced; feeds the registries from exercise 03.
- Rater-calibration standard names a guideline owner, an onboarding pass threshold, an ongoing reliability metric and cadence, and a calibration owner.
- Labelling budget is one page, finance-grade, with a trade-off ladder and a defined budget source.
- Quarterly review agenda is one page, time-boxed, with a chair, participants, and a memo output.
- A peer AI Engineering Manager (or a finance partner) can read the four artefacts and either approve the budget line or ask three sharp questions — not "I don't understand what this is."
- The flywheel plan's throughput numbers, the budget's capacity numbers, and the rater-calibration standard's reliability targets are internally consistent (the budget's labels-per-hour × capacity = the plan's claimed throughput).

## Stretch goals

- **Pilot the flywheel for one quarter.** Run Capture → Label → Version → Consume end-to-end on a narrow slice (e.g., 200 labelled traces, one new eval case family, one judge recalibration). At the end, run the quarterly review for real. Produce a one-page retrospective: throughput vs. target, reliability vs. target, incidents attributable to not-yet-flywheel-captured cases. Refine the plan.
- **Red-team-to-corpus closed-loop audit.** Go back through the last two quarters of red-team exercise outputs (if your organisation runs them). For each finding, trace: is there a corpus case for this finding? Is an eval exercising it? Did the release gate catch it? Write a one-page audit. Teams new to the discipline usually find a majority of findings unclosed; the exercise is to close them this quarter.
- **Vendor-selection memo.** If your labelling spend is large enough to justify a vendor relationship, produce the one-page vendor-selection memo your finance / procurement partner needs: short-list of 2–3 vendors (research the market), evaluation criteria (quality on a calibration pilot, DPA posture, capacity, pricing), and a recommendation. Stay pragmatic — the ideal vendor is the one that hits your κ target at your price, not the one with the best sales deck.
