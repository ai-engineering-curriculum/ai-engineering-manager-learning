## Overview

This track teaches the *engineering-manager operating system* for teams that build AI products and agents at level 45: taking over a 10-30-engineer AI engineering team → hiring and leveling across the applied-AI, agentic, eval, RAG, fine-tune, and senior-IC sub-ladders (R1) → roadmapping and planning non-deterministic work with confidence-banded ETAs and capability-shift replanning (R2) → running an eval-driven SDLC where prompt + trace + judge review is a code-review requirement and eval deltas gate deploy (R3 + R12 + R13) → owning the vendor + model portfolio across providers, pricing, DPAs, and fallback routing (R4) → operating the model + data + safety release triad as the ship/no-ship gate (R5 + R15) → leading AI-specific incident response and blameless postmortems that feed back into eval fixtures (R6) → specialising performance management, growth, and anti-atrophy practice for AI engineering ladders (R7) → setting team shape, span, and the AI-specific operating cadence including the IC AI-assist stack (R8 + R14) → owning team budget, FinOps-for-inference, build-vs-buy, and portfolio allocation (R9) → running cross-functional partnership with governance, security, legal, product, and AI infra (R10 + R16 + R17) → and leading the executive-communication story that defends investment, narrates regressions honestly, and maintains demo discipline (R11) — then positioning the role for the next step on the ladder.

It is a **manager track at level 45**, positioned in the **AI Engineering family**. The differentiator against [`ai-augmented-team-manager-learning`](https://github.com/ai-workforce-curriculum/ai-augmented-team-manager-learning) (level 40, AI Workforce family) is *managing a team that **builds** AI vs. a team that **uses** AI*: this curriculum assumes the team-adoption operating system from that sibling-below and layers the engineering-building scope on top — eval-gated SDLC, vendor portfolio, release triad, AI-specific incident response, leveling across AI-engineering sub-ladders. The differentiator against [`ai-enablement-lead-learning`](https://github.com/ai-workforce-curriculum/ai-enablement-lead-learning) (level 45, AI Workforce family, sibling) is *function-level engineering vs. org-wide enablement*: this manager runs an engineering team; the enablement lead runs the CoE and literacy program the organization plugs into. The differentiator against [`ai-infra-team-lead-learning`](https://github.com/ai-infra-curriculum/ai-infra-team-lead-learning) (peer manager, AI Infra ladder) is *application-layer engineering vs. infra engineering*: this manager leads teams shipping AI product and agents; the infra team lead leads teams shipping model-serving, GPU capacity, and training platform. The differentiator against [`agentic-systems-architect-learning`](https://github.com/ai-engineering-curriculum/agentic-systems-architect-learning) (level 48) and [`principal-ml-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ml-engineering-curriculum/principal-ml-engineer-learning) (level 48) is *management-track vs. staff-plus IC-track*: this role partners with staff-plus ICs; it does not replace them. The differentiator against [`chief-ai-officer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/chief-ai-officer-learning) (level 70) is *function-level vs. executive*: this role runs the engineering operating system for a team or group; the CAIO sets enterprise AI strategy on top of it.

Total planned commitment: **176 hours across 12 modules** + **140 hours across 3 projects** = **~316 hours**.

## Ownership rule

Following the project-wide ownership rule, this curriculum:

- **Owns** the engineering-management operating system for teams that build AI products: hiring and leveling across the AI engineering ladder; roadmapping under model non-determinism; eval-driven SDLC + eval-gated release; vendor + model portfolio with fallback; the model + data + safety release triad; AI-specific incident response and blameless postmortems; performance management and growth specialised for AI engineering levels; team shape, span, and the AI-specific operating cadence (including the IC AI-assist stack commissioning); budget, FinOps for inference, and build-vs-buy; cross-functional partnership with governance, security, legal, product, and AI infra; executive communication under model uncertainty.
- **Defers down** to [`ai-augmented-team-manager-learning`](https://github.com/ai-workforce-curriculum/ai-augmented-team-manager-learning) (level 40) for the team-scope AI-adoption fundamentals and general engineering-management rituals this role assumes (1:1s, feedback, hiring loop mechanics, team-adoption roadmap, review standards at the team level). **Defers down** to the AI Engineering IC tracks — [`applied-ai-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-engineering-curriculum/applied-ai-engineer-learning) (level 30), [`agentic-ai-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-engineering-curriculum/agentic-ai-engineer-learning) (level 30), [`ai-eval-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-engineering-curriculum/ai-eval-engineer-learning) (level 30), [`rag-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-engineering-curriculum/rag-engineer-learning) (level 30), [`fine-tuning-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-engineering-curriculum/fine-tuning-engineer-learning) (level 30), [`llm-application-developer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-engineering-curriculum/llm-application-developer-learning) (level 20), [`senior-agentic-ai-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-engineering-curriculum/senior-agentic-ai-engineer-learning) (level 40) — for the IC craft this role hires, levels, and reviews work from; to [`ai-assisted-software-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-engineering-curriculum/ai-assisted-software-engineer-learning) (level 20) for the IC-level AI-assist stack this manager commissions.
- **Defers sideways** to [`ai-enablement-lead-learning`](https://github.com/ai-workforce-curriculum/ai-enablement-lead-learning) (level 45, AI Workforce family) for org-wide enablement scope (CoE, policy co-authoring, non-engineer literacy); to [`agent-platform-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-engineering-curriculum/agent-platform-engineer-learning) (level 40) for internal agent-platform IC build depth; to [`agentic-safety-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-engineering-curriculum/agentic-safety-engineer-learning) (level 40) for frontier-agent red-team methodology; to [`forward-deployed-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-engineering-curriculum/forward-deployed-engineer-learning) (level 35) for customer-embedded AI build when the team ships that shape of work; to [`ai-infra-team-lead-learning`](https://github.com/ai-infra-curriculum/ai-infra-team-lead-learning) (peer manager, level 45, AI Infra ladder) for AI-infra manager scope when the team is infra-shaped. **Defers sideways** to the AI Governance family — [`ai-risk-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/ai-risk-engineer-learning) (peer, level 25) for engineered risk / red-team / guardrail depth this manager commissions; [`ai-evaluation-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/ai-evaluation-engineer-learning) (peer, level 35) for release-assurance / audit-trail methodology this manager consumes; [`senior-ai-governance-architect-learning`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/senior-ai-governance-architect-learning) (peer, level 50) for control-library architecture; [`head-of-ai-governance-learning`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/head-of-ai-governance-learning) (peer, level 60) for governance program leadership.
- **Defers up** to [`agentic-systems-architect-learning`](https://github.com/ai-engineering-curriculum/agentic-systems-architect-learning) (level 48) and [`principal-ml-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ml-engineering-curriculum/principal-ml-engineer-learning) (level 48) for staff-plus technical architecture scope this role partners with but does not own as IC; to [`chief-ai-officer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/chief-ai-officer-learning) (level 70) for enterprise AI strategy, portfolio P&L, board-level external positioning, and M&A.
- **Out of scope** to legal counsel (this role does not deliver legal opinion), to procurement and finance (contract execution and billing mechanics), to IT identity teams (SSO / IAM implementation), and to security operations / DFIR (SOC-side investigation; this role feeds AI-specific signal to SecOps).

## Module summaries

### mod-101 — The AI Engineering Manager: Role, Ladder Position, and the Operating Contract
Position the level-45 AI Engineering Manager against `ai-augmented-team-manager` (level 40, AI Workforce family — the team-management sibling that owns the general AI-adoption operating system), `ai-enablement-lead` (level 45, sibling), `ai-infra-team-lead` (peer manager on the Infra ladder), `agentic-systems-architect` and `principal-ml-engineer` (level 48) above on the IC architecture track, and `chief-ai-officer` (level 70) at the executive altitude. Read the role's operating-contract surface as the union of general engineering-management canon (Fournier, Grove, Larson, Lopp) and AI-engineering-specific scope. Diagram handoff contracts to the IC tracks this role manages, the governance peers whose evidence it consumes, and the executive level above. Author the 90-day entry plan — team audit, model + vendor inventory, eval + incident inventory, prompt + judge registry audit, stakeholder tour, first commitments.

### mod-102 — Hiring, Leveling, and Interview Loops for AI Engineering Roles
Author the role matrix across applied-AI, agentic, eval, RAG, fine-tune, LLM developer, senior / staff IC — grounded in Dropbox / Google / GitLab public leveling exemplars and specialised to the AI-engineering families. Write JDs that name concrete artifacts an IC owns per level (traces reviewed, eval suites commissioned, prompts registered, incidents led). Design interview loops that pair general SWE rounds with AI-specific craft — prompt + trace + judge review, model-selection reasoning, eval design, non-deterministic debugging, LLM + retrieval + tool-use system design. Run calibration across L2..L5 of the IC ladder and across the staff-plus altitude. Design sourcing and pipeline plans; avoid the trap of hiring a single 'AI-first-ever' superstar who refuses to work the eval and incident surface.

### mod-103 — Roadmapping and Planning Non-Deterministic Work
Build quarterly and annual roadmaps where ETAs carry confidence bands and the plan assumes mid-quarter model-capability shifts. Design the prototype → MVP → GA promotion gate — what evidence (eval delta, user-research signal, cost ceiling, safety floor) moves work forward, what blocks it. Pair capacity planning with provider release cadence — model deprecations, capability unlocks, pricing resets — reading Anthropic RSP / OpenAI Preparedness / DeepMind FSF as planning inputs, not policy this role owns. Design the research-to-production handoff (R16) with ML research scientists or external research teams: acceptance gate (reproducibility, eval scope, documentation, on-call readiness), owner-of-record transition. Operate the discovery / delivery / incident mix at a manager's altitude.

### mod-104 — Eval-Driven Delivery and the Eval-Gated SDLC
Commission an eval suite (not build it IC-level) and decide which evals gate PRs vs. which gate releases — grounded in NIST GenAI Profile, OWASP LLM Top 10, OpenAI Evals, Inspect, promptfoo, Ragas, DeepEval, LangSmith, Langfuse, Braintrust, Arize patterns. Make prompt + trace + judge review a code-review requirement (R12) and operate the prompt / judge / golden-dataset registries as first-class artifacts. Design offline + online eval cadence (pre-merge, canary, A/B, shadow, continuous production monitoring); set eval-delta thresholds that gate deploy and that page on-call. Own the data flywheel (R13) — how labelling / preference / red-team data gets collected, versioned, consumed by eval and fine-tune, and funded. Carry the standard that an eval-change PR touches the registry, the eval report, and the release note.

### mod-105 — Vendor and Model Portfolio Selection
Run the model + provider portfolio across the current frontier (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Mistral, Cohere, Meta open-weights, specialists) on context window, pricing (input / output / cached / batch / fine-tune), latency, throughput, SLA, DPA / SOC2, residency, enterprise features. Make defensible model-selection calls per feature and design fallback + multi-model routing. Partner with procurement on enterprise contracts — read DPAs, know which clauses matter (training-on-data, retention, residency, sub-processors), raise redlines; route legal opinion to counsel. Position open-weights / self-host honestly — when cost / data / latency justifies self-host, when it does not — partnering with [`ai-infra-team-lead`](https://github.com/ai-infra-curriculum/ai-infra-team-lead-learning) on serving. Build the quarterly portfolio review cadence with explicit decommissioning criteria.

### mod-106 — Safety, Guardrails, and the Model + Data + Safety Release Triad
Own safety as a product requirement — write guardrails as product specs (input filters, output validators, tool-invocation policy, HITL checkpoints, refusal policy, content classifier choice) an applied-ai-engineer IC can build against — grounded in OWASP LLM Top 10, NIST GenAI Profile, NeMo Guardrails, Llama Guard. Commission red-team work from [`ai-risk-engineer`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/ai-risk-engineer-learning) (peer, level 25) and [`agentic-safety-engineer`](https://github.com/ai-engineering-curriculum/agentic-safety-engineer-learning) (peer, level 40) — author the brief, review the findings, route the fixes. Operate the model + data + safety release triad as the manager's gate. Shape the agent / tool-use authorization story at the product boundary (R15) — OAuth, scoped service identity, MCP contract, HITL checkpoints — where IC craft defers sideways. Translate EU AI Act Articles 14, 15, and 50 into engineering deliverables without taking legal positions.

### mod-107 — AI-Specific Incident Response and Blameless Postmortems
Design the on-call + incident path for AI-specific failures (jailbreak, prompt injection, hallucination, post-vendor-upgrade regression, judge drift, tool-use failure, embedding-store corruption, retrieval degradation, outage, moderation-classifier false-positive storm) — adapted from Google SRE Book, SRE Workbook, and Etsy blameless-postmortem practice. Operate incident command across the AI-shaped surface (TTD, TTM, kill switch, routing to safer model, prompt/judge/tool rollback, evidence preservation). Run blameless postmortems that produce permanent eval fixtures — every confirmed incident becomes a regression test and a judge case. Shape regulator-facing narratives where EU AI Act Article 73 applies; route SOC-side investigation to security operations / DFIR. Read AI Incident Database, OECD.AI, and the MIT AI Risk Repository as regular reference, not only after your own incident.

### mod-108 — Performance Management, Growth Paths, and Anti-Atrophy for AI Engineers
Specialise 1:1, feedback, and performance-review practice (from the engineering-management canon, not re-taught here) to AI engineering ladders — what 'good' looks like at applied-AI L2/L3/L4/L5, at agentic / eval / RAG / fine-tune specialist altitudes, at staff IC. Author growth plans across sub-ladders including the IC-to-management transition (partnered with `ai-augmented-team-manager` level 40). Address anti-atrophy risk from over-reliance on frontier assistants: rotation across model + framework shifts, mandatory read-time against vendor cookbooks and provider release notes, a deliberate 'learn without the assistant' loop. Run calibration fairly under rapid technology drift. Design the feedback cadence for frontier-tooling-era seniors vs. juniors leveraging AI-assist responsibly. Position AI engineering credentials and conference / publication signal honestly.

### mod-109 — Team Shape, Span, and the AI Engineering Operating Cadence
Choose team shape (applied + eval + fine-tune split; vertical vs. horizontal; with/without embedded PM) using Larson's team-sizing framework specialised for AI-engineering workloads. Design the AI-specific ritual set — weekly model-watch, prompt + eval + trace review, incident replay, vendor-release review — and eliminate rituals that don't earn their place. Set the backlog + bug + experiment triage cadence appropriate for non-deterministic systems. Commission the IC-level AI-assist stack for the team (R14) — Cursor, Claude Code, Copilot / Workspace, Windsurf, Cody, Sweep — choose, roll out, measure productivity honestly (Dell'Acqua et al. jagged frontier; Peng et al. copilot study), set team policy. Operate span-of-control honestly at a level-45 altitude — when to add a tech-lead / staff-plus IC partner, when to split pods, when to coach a senior IC into the first-time-manager transition.

### mod-110 — Budget, FinOps for Inference, and Build-vs-Buy
Build the team's cost model across model-provider spend, labelling data, observability and eval tooling, training compute, infrastructure, and headcount; attribute cost per feature / per user / per workflow honestly. Project ROI with defensible value-realisation framing and confidence bands; publish misses with the reason, not only hits. Make build-vs-buy calls across the vendor-API / fine-tune / self-host axis. Run quarterly portfolio allocation across prototype / productise / wind-down. Monitor FinOps for inference — caching rates, batch utilisation, fallback routing, prompt-size discipline, context-window waste — the levers a manager can own; infra platform depth defers to ai-infra-inference-engineer and ai-infra-team-lead.

### mod-111 — Cross-Functional Partnership — Governance, Security, Legal, Product, Infra
Design the release-approval workflow with governance, legal, security, and privacy partners — sign-offs, evidence consumed (eval reports, red-team findings, model cards, data-flow diagrams, DPIA inputs), end-to-end timeline. Operate the interface with the AI Governance family — consume engineered-risk evidence from `ai-risk-engineer` (level 25), release-assurance / audit-trail from `ai-evaluation-engineer` (level 35), participate in the governance program owned by `head-of-ai-governance` (level 60) and `senior-ai-governance-architect` (level 50). Partner with Product under model uncertainty. Operate the interface with AI Infra — `ai-infra-team-lead` (peer manager, level 45) owns serving / capacity / GPU; this role is the customer, with shared SLOs and shared incident path (R17). Position the sibling relationship with `ai-enablement-lead` (level 45) and `ai-augmented-team-manager` (level 40). Route legal, procurement, IT identity, and SOC-side out.

### mod-112 — Executive Communication and Stakeholder Reporting
Produce weekly roll-ups, monthly deep-dives, and quarterly exec-ready summaries that make model-uncertainty and release-gate reasoning legible to leadership that does not read eval reports directly. Defend AI investment asks with empirical evidence (Dell'Acqua et al. jagged frontier; Peng et al. copilot productivity) referenced honestly. Narrate regressions honestly — publish misses with the reason. Maintain demo discipline — prevent the cherry-picked demo that pushes against the honest eval story in team artifacts, exec packets, and external-facing comms. Position the role's next step on the ladder — director / head of AI engineering / chief-ai-officer (level 70), sideways to ai-enablement-lead (level 45) or principal-ml-engineer / agentic-systems-architect (level 48) — and the deliberate-practice plan to get there.
